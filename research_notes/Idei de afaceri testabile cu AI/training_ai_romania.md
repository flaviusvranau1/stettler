## 1. What Romanian companies and institutions pay for AI training, and who the providers are

### Takeaway
In 2026, published prices for open-enrolment AI workshops in Romania cluster around 650–1,300 lei per participant for 6–10 hours. Some of these prices are + TVA. The top of the market is about EUR 540 + TVA per person per day. Long online courses that are ANC-authorized or MEC-accredited (25–75 hours) sell for about 975–1,290 lei. No Romanian provider publishes a per-day rate for in-house training, so in-house work is priced by quote. Public buyers buy small AI trainings through SEAP direct purchases. One school paid 37,100 lei in 2025.

### Inferences
- **Price bands**, based on about 8 public Romanian listings from 2026:
  - Short open-enrolment AI workshops (6–10 hours): about 650–1,300 lei per participant. The top of the market is Leadder at EUR 540 + TVA, about 2,700 lei + TVA at an assumed ~5 lei/EUR.
  - Long certified online courses (25–75 hours): about 975–1,290 lei. That is only about 14–50 lei per participant-hour, because buyers are paying for the certificate or credits.
  - Short workshops work out to about 80–160 lei per participant-hour. For example, 800 lei for 8–10 hours is 80–100 lei per hour, and 1,250 lei for 8–10 hours is 125–156 lei per hour.
- **In-house day rate:** no Romanian per-day rate is public, and in-house work is quoted (NobleProg, dbvmt, Bittnet). Multiplying per-person prices by a group of 10–16 gives roughly 8,000–20,000 lei per day as an upper reference. In-house deals are usually discounted, and this figure is not evidence-based.
- **Benchmark for state-funded mass training:** about 1,800 lei per civil servant (54.05 million lei / 30,000), including testing and certification.
- **Public buyers:** small AI trainings go through SEAP direct purchase, far below the ceiling. Being listed in the SEAP catalogue matters more than winning tenders. One solo trainer prices its public-institution workshop about 56% above its business workshop (1,250 vs 800 lei), which suggests public buyers accept a higher price per head. This is one data point.
- **Foreign group prices** (EUR 750 per half-day for up to 10 people; EUR 1,100 per 2.5 hours for up to 25) imply roughly EUR 190–440 per delivery hour in richer markets.
- **Implementation is far bigger than training:** about 1.88 million lei for RPA and AI in Blaj, against tens of thousands of lei for courses. Training may work best as a way in to automation projects.

### Gaps

## 2. Demand drivers: AI Act Article 4, Romanian adoption statistics, and public funding

### Takeaway
Article 4 has applied since 2 Feb 2025. It was softened on 27 July 2026 by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the "Digital Omnibus on AI"). It is now an obligation of effort: providers and deployers must "take measures to support the development" of AI literacy. No certificate is required, but documentation is expected. In Romania, the sanctions regime is not yet working: ANCOM says it cannot check or fine anyone until a national law is passed, and none had been found as of August 2026.

Romania has the lowest enterprise AI adoption in the EU (5.21% in 2025, against an EU-27 average of 19.95%). Most use is employee-initiated and has no company policy behind it.

The big public digital-skills money (PNRR) ended in mid-2026. EU money for employee training (PEO) flows through authorized training providers. Teachers have a dedicated, funded channel.

### Cited Findings

#### 2a. Article 4: what it requires now
- [OFFICIAL, via commentary] **Original wording (2024 text):** providers and deployers must "take measures to ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy" among their staff and others who operate AI on their behalf. This applied from 2 Feb 2025 to 26 July 2026. Sources: [lawandtechnology.eu](https://lawandtechnology.eu/en/ai-literacy-digital-omnibus-article-4-ai-act/); [artificialintelligenceact.eu (RO)](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ro/article/4/)
- [OFFICIAL] **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 ("Digital Omnibus on AI")** was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026. Article 1(5) replaces Article 4 in full. The duty's start date did not move: it has applied since 2 February 2025. Source: [EUR-Lex OJ L 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AL_202601744). The PDF was not opened; the details come from [ki-campus.org](https://ki-campus.org/en/blog/eu-ai-act-ai-omnibus-recalibrates-article-4-ai-literacy-remains-mandatory), [dastra.eu](https://www.dastra.eu/en/blog/ai-literacy-why-the-omnibus-changed-the-rules-of-the-game-without-changing-the/60562) and [lmspedia.org](https://lmspedia.org/eu-ai-act-regulation-2026-1744-training-deadlines/).
- [OFFICIAL, quoted by secondary sources] **New wording:** providers and deployers shall "take measures to support the development of AI literacy" among their staff and others who operate or use AI systems on their behalf.
  - The measures should take into account technical knowledge, experience, education and training, the context of use, and the persons affected.
  - The text expressly says there is no obligation to ensure a particular literacy level for each individual.
  - It applies to all AI systems, not only high-risk ones.
  - Sources: [lawandtechnology.eu](https://lawandtechnology.eu/en/ai-literacy-digital-omnibus-article-4-ai-act/); [ainora.lt](https://ainora.lt/blog/eu-ai-act-article-4-ai-literacy); [dastra.eu](https://www.dastra.eu/en/blog/ai-literacy-why-the-omnibus-changed-the-rules-of-the-game-without-changing-the/60562)
- [COMMENTARY] Commentators read this as a move from an obligation of result to an obligation of effort, where the "sufficient level" benchmark is gone. Sources: [dastra.eu](https://www.dastra.eu/en/blog/ai-literacy-why-the-omnibus-changed-the-rules-of-the-game-without-changing-the/60562); [getactready.com](https://getactready.com/blog/eu-ai-act-ai-literacy-rule-softened-omnibus); [casys.ai](https://casys.ai/blog/digital-omnibus-article-4-what-changed)
- [COMMENTARY] The November 2025 Commission proposal would have moved the duty onto the Commission and Member States and left companies only "encouraged". The adopted text keeps a softened duty on providers and deployers and gives the Commission and Member States a supporting role, especially for SMEs. Sources: [ki-campus.org](https://ki-campus.org/en/blog/eu-ai-act-ai-omnibus-recalibrates-article-4-ai-literacy-remains-mandatory); [insideprivacy.com (on the proposal)](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/digital-omnibus-package-series-european-commissions-proposal-to-revise-the-eus-ai-rules/)
- [OFFICIAL, as summarised by law firms] **The Commission's AI literacy Q&A** (first published May 2025; 7 May per one source) says:
  - No certificate or outside accreditation is required.
  - Organisations should keep internal records of the measures they take.
  - The format is open, but relying only on instructions for use or a manual is likely to be insufficient, especially for high-risk systems.
  - The duty covers contractors and service providers who act on the deployer's behalf.
  - Measures should be tailored to role, sector and risk.
  - A deployer is any entity that uses AI under its authority, except for personal non-professional use. The AI Act definition in Art. 3(4) also covers public authorities; that article was not fetched separately.
  - Sources: [Commission Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers); [DLA Piper, 27 May 2025](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2025/05/eu-ai-act-update-ai-literacy-questions-and-answers); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/european-commission-publishes-q-a-on-ai-literacy); [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102kazh/ai-literacy-european-commission-publishes-faq)
- [COMMENTARY] The duty reaches employees who only use ChatGPT for work. Source: [Shibolet](https://www.shibolet.com/en/but-what-if-i-only-use-chatgpt-update-on-new-ai-literacy-guidelines-by-eu-ai-office/)
- [OFFICIAL, via commentary] The Commission keeps a repository of 40+ AI-literacy practices from companies and public bodies. Adopting one does not create a presumption of compliance. Sources: [Commission Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers); [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102kazh/ai-literacy-european-commission-publishes-faq)
- [COMMENTARY][1-SRC] The Q&A was reportedly updated on 27 July 2026 to cover measures, formats, certificates, records and examples. Source: [aiactblog.nl](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/ai-act-monitor/article-4-measures-duty-updated-guidance)
- [OFFICIAL, via commentary] **Enforcement and fines:**
  - National market surveillance authorities enforce Article 4, not the AI Office.
  - Supervision starts on 3 August 2026 according to the Q&A; other sources say 2 August 2026.
  - Fines come from national law. Article 4 is not in the fine tiers of Art. 99(4), so the often-quoted EUR 15 million ceiling does not apply to it.
  - Sources: [Commission Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers); [ainora.lt](https://ainora.lt/blog/eu-ai-act-article-4-ai-literacy); [lawandtechnology.eu](https://lawandtechnology.eu/en/ai-literacy-digital-omnibus-article-4-ai-act/)
- [OFFICIAL][1-SRC] The Commission's Romanian-language policy page still showed the original wording. Source: [digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/ro](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/ro/policies/ai-talent-skills-and-literacy)
- [COMMENTARY] The separate Art. 26(2) duty is unchanged: deployers of high-risk systems must assign staff who are competent and trained. One commentator says obligations for standalone high-risk systems now start in December 2027. Source: [lmspedia.org](https://lmspedia.org/eu-ai-act-regulation-2026-1744-training-deadlines/)

#### 2b. Enforcement in Romania
- [PRESS/COMMENTARY] **Government memorandum of 12 March 2026** names:

### Inferences
- **The compliance driver is weak in October 2026.** Article 4 is now an effort obligation with no certificate requirement, and Romania has no sanctions regime yet. So "AI Act training" works more as a sales hook than as a requirement that is being enforced. Still, the duty exists and documentation is expected. A short, role-based, documented session plus an internal AI-use policy is the most defensible "minimum compliance" product.
- **The underlying gap is large, but willingness to pay is unproven.** Enterprise adoption is 5.21%. 47.4% of employers report AI use with no policy. Only 6% of employees use generative AI daily. In the public sector, 54% of staff use AI but only 23% of institutions invest in it. This points to demand for "safe use + practical skills" packages. But low adoption also means few buyers who already recognise the need.
- **Timing of public money.** PNRR digital-skills money was largely spent by mid-2026. PEO 2024 digital-skills projects (running 2025–2027) and STEP-LLL 2026 projects (likely starting 2027) will need trainers. That money flows through authorized providers, which makes subcontracting the realistic way in.
- **The Law 76 subsidy is a possible selling point**, since an employer can recover 50% of an authorized course. It only works with authorized providers, and there is no data on how much it is used.
- **The teacher segment is funded and active** (a 1,500-lei annual bonus and a 15-ECTS requirement). But it needs MEC accreditation, and existing accredited providers already sell at about 975–1,025 lei.
- **Free offers from Microsoft, Google, EU-funded projects, Raiffeisen and ANFP compete directly** with generic introductory workshops.

### Gaps
- No survey or case found of Romanian employers buying training specifically because of Article 4.
- No evidence that the Romanian implementing law passed after early August 2026.
- Could not confirm the exact final wording of Article 4 from EUR-Lex, or what the July 2026 Q&A update changed.
- The 2026 status of PNRR I19 (ADR SME scheme), of the ANFP AI programme, and the final outcome of I16 are unknown.
- STEP-LLL: eligible applicants, and whether AI or "digital" training qualifies as a "critical technology", are not confirmed.
- Eurostat's 2025 figures on individuals' use of generative AI in Romania could not be searched because the budget ran out.
- Training funding in the Regional Programme Nord-Vest, and free training from European Digital Innovation Hubs in Nord-Vest, were not researched.

## 3. Credentials and channels: ANC authorization, subcontracting, trainer rates

### Takeaway
No source found says ANC authorization is needed to sell ordinary non-certified in-company workshops. Authorization is the key that opens nationally recognised certificates, the Law 76 50% subsidy, EU-funded calls (where applicants must be authorized providers), and apparently VAT-exempt pricing. The cost and time to get authorized could not be verified. Hourly rates seen in publicly funded projects run from 80 lei gross (2020, trainer) to 119–154 lei gross (2024–2025, management and expert roles).

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] Under OG 129/2000, authorization is required for programmes that end in nationally recognised certificates. This comes from a 2018 clarification; attribution to this document is inferred. Source: [Senat.ro (parliamentary answer)](https://senat.ro/PDFIntrebari/b%20r%2001%2009%20dinica%203476%20a.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL][1-SRC] Authorization is granted **per programme** by county and Bucharest authorization commissions within the social payments and inspection agencies (AJPIS/AJPIS București). ### Inferences
- **Three possible paths:**
  - **A. Sell non-accredited in-house workshops directly to private firms**, issuing a certificate of participation. No source found that requires ANC authorization for this, but that is not explicitly confirmed.
  - **B. Subcontract to, or partner with, authorized providers, universities or consortia** to reach PEO, Law 76, ANOFM and public-sector budgets. Rates seen in such projects are 80–154 lei gross per hour (2020–2025).
  - **C. Get your own ANC authorization.** It is per programme and valid for 4 years. Its cost and time are unverified, and it may be heavy for a side business with under 5,000 lei and 10–15 hours a week.
- **Income arithmetic:** 80–154 lei/hour × 43–65 hours/month (10–15 h/week) = about 3,400–10,000 lei gross per month, but only at full booking. Real booked hours in projects are usually lower, and no data on this was found.
- **The VAT signal:** if authorized adult training is VAT-exempt, authorized competitors have about a 21% price advantage when selling to buyers who cannot reclaim VAT, such as public institutions, schools and VAT-exempt firms. The 21% figure assumes the standard VAT rate in 2026 and was not verified here.
- **The public channel needs SEAP catalogue registration** (and probably a qualified e-signature, which was not verified) rather than ANC authorization, at least for private-label workshops bought by direct purchase.

### Gaps
- The ANC programme-authorization fee, the documents required, and processing time in 2026 were **not found**, and the search budget ran out before this could be searched again.
- The cost and duration of the "Formator" trainer certificate that EU projects usually require were not found.
- Hourly rate caps or unit costs for trainers in the PEO 2021–2027 guides were not found.
- Typical contract form (PFA or SRL service contract versus a part-time project role) and **payment terms or delays** for subcontracted trainers were not found.
- The exact VAT exemption rule (Fiscal Code art. 292) was not verified.
- Whether a CAEN 8559 company needs anything beyond normal registration to sell training was not verified.

## 5. Competition and what differentiates trainers who sell well

### Takeaway
The generic "ChatGPT/Copilot for employees" segment is crowded: 20+ Romanian providers or offers turned up in about 30 searches. It is also undercut by free programmes from Microsoft, Google, EU-funded projects, Raiffeisen with HBS, and ANFP's PNRR training. The positioning patterns visible in 2026 are:
- packaging and pricing by segment (public institutions, educators, tax teams, top management);
- accreditation that unlocks budgets (ANC "curs autorizat", MEC ECTS/CPT);
- hands-on use of several tools;
- links to implementation work.

No data shows which of these actually sells best.

### Inferences
- **Generic introductory "AI for employees" content is a crowded, price-pressured and partly free category.** The open segments look like these:
  - (a) hands-on automation (n8n or workflow) in Romanian, which produces working workflows during the session;
  - (b) sector packages for local SMEs, municipalities or schools in Nord-Vest, priced through SEAP direct purchase or company budgets;
  - (c) training bundled with a paid follow-on implementation.
- **Fit for a developer who builds LLM apps and n8n automations.** The automation angle is the most differentiated asset compared with the many prompt-focused trainers seen. Teaching experience with adults is a plus. Accreditation-dependent segments (civil servants needing ANC certificates, teachers needing MEC credits) are reachable mainly through partners. This is an inference from the pattern of offers, not measured performance.
- **Cluj and Oradea already have AI-training providers. None were found in Bistrița-Năsăud.** That could mean less local competition or less local demand; this research cannot tell which.
