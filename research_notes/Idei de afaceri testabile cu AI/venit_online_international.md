# Online / international income models for a solo AI-automation & web developer: evidence as of October 2026

Method note (applies to every section): compiled 2026-10-08. The tooling constraint allowed only WebSearch result summaries, with no page fetches. 36 searches completed. Six more planned searches were blocked because the shared session web-search budget ran out: primary Reddit threads, GigRadar reply-rate data, MicroConf churn and time-to-profit, review-management pricing, A2P 10DLC SMS fees and speed-to-lead studies. Each relevant Gaps list names them. All figures come from search summaries, so read each one as snippet-level unless several sources agree.

Evidence tags:
- **[OFFICIAL]**: data published by the platform, regulator or company itself.
- **[INDEP]**: an independent dataset, or analysis by a law firm or trade press.
- **[VENDOR]**: a company that sells a competing or adjacent product.
- **[GURU]**: course or agency-marketing content.
- **[ANECDOTE]**: an individual's self-reported result.
- **[1-SRC]**: only one source was found.

## 1. Freelance demand and rates 2025–2026 (n8n/Make/Zapier, AI agents, RAG/LLM chatbots, Next.js) and platform fees

### Takeaway
On Upwork, the two fastest-growing coding/web-dev skills by freelancer earnings in 2025 were **AI integration (+178% YoY)** and **AI chatbot development (+71% YoY)**. Total Upwork job postings, however, **fell for four straight quarters through Q2 2026** (a third-party tracker), and the n8n-tagged niche is small: about **600–650 postings a month**, with price pressure at the low end. Third-party guides put mid-level automation work at roughly **$40–$100/h**. Romania and Eastern-Europe contract benchmarks are about **€20–70/h**. Upwork charges a **variable 0–15% fee**, plus about **$0.90–$2.40 in Connects per proposal**.

### Cited Findings

#### Upwork fees and bidding costs (2026)
- [OFFICIAL] The freelancer service fee is **variable, 0%–15% per contract**. The rate is shown before you submit a proposal or accept an offer, and it stays fixed for that contract. Any Hire and Payroll carry no fee. Direct Contracts (clients you bring to Upwork) cost **5% on Basic** and **0% for active Freelancer Plus members**; Plus costs **$19.99/month**. — [Upwork Help Center: Freelancer Service Fee](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211062538-Learn-about-the-Freelancer-Service-Fee) (undated snippet, seen Oct 2026)
- [OFFICIAL, via search summary] Upwork's own 2026 pricing explainer lists Freelancer Plus at **$19.99/month, with 100 Connects and access to Uma (its AI assistant)**. The summary also says Plus "drops the freelancer service fee to 0%". **This conflicts with the Help Center snippet**, which limits the 0% to Direct Contracts. Verify before relying on it. — [Upwork: Is Upwork free? (2026)](https://www.upwork.com/resources/is-upwork-free)
- [3rd-party, rival platform] Plus costs $19.99 on the web and **$27/month through the Apple App Store** (Jan 2026). — [Jobbers](https://www.jobbers.io/should-i-pay-for-upwork-plus-vs-free-jobbers-account-complete-roi-calculator-2026/)

  - Sources: [golance (2026)](https://golance.com/blogs/upwork-fees-explained-2026); [HireInSouth](https://www.hireinsouth.com/post/how-much-does-upwork-cost); [Vortenza](https://www.vortenza.com/guides/upwork-fees-2026)

### Inferences
- **Positioning.** His stack (Next.js/React/Node, Claude RAG/OCR, n8n) maps onto Upwork's two fastest-growing coding categories (AI integration, chatbot development) and onto the largest category by volume (full-stack). "n8n automator" on its own targets a niche of about 600 postings a month with low-budget competition, such as the $5–$20/h listing with 50+ bids. The positioning the evidence supports better is "AI integration / RAG / document-AI + full-stack".
- **Market direction.** Total Upwork postings are shrinking (GigRadar) while earnings in AI categories grow (Upwork). That suggests fewer but higher-value AI jobs. Competition per job was not measured.
- **Unit costs.** These are my arithmetic from the cited unit prices:
  - On a $1,000 contract he nets about $850–$1,000 after the 0–15% fee.
  - 40 proposals a month cost about 240–640 Connects, or roughly $36–$96.
  - Freelancer Plus at $19.99 includes 100 Connects (about $15 of value) plus 0% on Direct Contracts.
- **Rate expectations.** A plausible band for a Romania-based mid/senior developer on international platforms:
  - Starting out: about **$30–$60/h**, anchored on the Eastern Europe median of about $52/h and the Hays range of €26–68/h.
  - With AI-specific reviews: rising toward **$60–$100/h** (Ad Snipper's mid-level band).
  - Don't plan on the $95–$235/h figures; they come from marketing content.

### Gaps
- No Upwork-official data on job counts for n8n, Make, Zapier or RAG, on hourly rates by level, or on rates achieved by Eastern-European freelancers. No data on how long a new profile takes to win its first contract.
- GigRadar's reply rates and cost-per-reply by category would quantify proposal economics; that search was blocked by the budget.
- No Contra demand data, no Malt Romania data, and nothing on what Toptal actually pays freelancers.
- Fee history conflicts: sources disagree on whether the fee before 2025 was a flat 10% or tiered 20/10/5%.

## 2. The "AI automation agency" retainer model: real outcomes vs course-seller hype

### Takeaway
I found **no survey, dataset or audited case** that measures outcomes for solo "AI automation agencies". The quantified evidence is of two kinds:
- **Vendor and agency price guides.** SMB retainers are most often **$500–$1,500/month plus a $1,500–$5,000 setup fee**, or about $1,000–$3,500/month for 2–3 workflows.
- **A secondary digest of Reddit threads.** Realistic operators land **1–2 low-four-figure retainers early**, and big "$30k/month" claims are mostly course revenue.

Getting clients, not the tooling, is the bottleneck these sources name. Cold-email reply rates average **0.45%–3.43%**, depending on how they are measured. **No public churn data exists.**

### Cited Findings

#### Price points (all seller-written, 2026)
- [VENDOR] Taskip pricing guide:
  - SMB retainers **$500–$1,500/month**, plus a one-time setup of **$1,500–$5,000**.
  - Multi-system work $1,500–$4,000/month; enterprise $3,000–$8,000/month.
  - Retainers usually cover monitoring, prompt adjustments, bug fixes, small add-ons and reporting.
  - Source: [Taskip pricing guide](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/)
- [VENDOR] Two or three workflows run about **$1,000–$3,500/month**. — [Taskip cost guide](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-cost/); [Cursive Media](https://cursivemedia.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-cost)
  - The Taskip cost guide adds:
    - How hard systems are to connect is the main price driver.
    - Exit terms usually give 30 days' notice after the first term.
    - Plans sold as "unlimited" are often quietly capped.
    - It presents $2,800–$7,000 as a "median retainer" without a sample size.
- [VENDOR] Managed SMB systems cost $300–$3,000/month, and most single-system setups fall at **$500–$1,500**. — [Evolvai Agents](https://evolvaiagents.com/blog/how-much-does-an-ai-automation-agency-cost-in-2026-real-pricing-breakdown/)
- [VENDOR] Retainers by niche:
  - Home services: $2,000–$4,000/month.
  - Dental/medical: $2,000–$5,000.
  - Legal: $2,000–$4,500.
  - Individual real-estate agents: $1,500–$3,500.
  - Source: [Datastaq](https://datastaqai.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-niches-small-business)
- [VENDOR] At the floor, upkeep for a basic FAQ bot runs **$150–$500/month** after a $1,500–$3,500 build. — [Mintec](https://mintec.co/blog/ai-automation-small-business-revenue/)
- [VENDOR] Higher-end quotes:
  - Consultant-style retainers of **$2,000–$8,000/month for about 10–25 hours of work**, i.e. roughly $80–$320/h effective (my arithmetic). — [AI Essentials](https://aiessentials.us/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-hire-an-ai-consultant-for-my-small)
  - One agency's own retainer is $4,000/month and includes 1–2 new automations plus maintenance. — [Brothers Automate](https://brothersautomate.com/how-much-does-ai-automation-cost)
- [INDEP survey of general agencies, not solo AI automators] Productive.io:
  - Its 2025 survey of more than 180 agencies found **about a third had faced client requests for AI-related discounts**.
  - A six-month follow-up of 174 agencies found **no consensus pricing model** for AI-era work.
  - 2026 coverage says **$10k/month AI retainers exist but are uncommon**, and the cheapest providers quote a few thousand a month for light setups built on ready-made tools.
  - Sources: [Productive 2025](https://productive.io/blog/agencies-in-the-ai-era/); [Productive pulse report](https://productive.io/reports/agencies-in-the-ai-era-pulse-report/); [Technology.org, 2026-04-23](https://www.technology.org/2026/04/23/inside-the-productive-io-survey-what-ai-agencies-actually-charge-in-2026/)

#### Failure reasons and churn
- [3rd-party digest] Two named failure reasons; the suggested fix is a single offer:
  - The niche is crowded with people selling the "agency dream". The resulting buyer skepticism **lengthens sales cycles**.
  - **Overextension** across many offers means never getting good at one while the support load piles up.
  - Source: [ciela.ai, "worth it"](https://ciela.ai/blogs/is-starting-an-ai-automation-agency-worth-it-reddit)
- [VENDOR] Churn reasons for AI-receptionist retainers in home services (no churn rates given):

## 3. Selling AI services remotely to US small businesses (AI phone receptionist, review management, speed-to-lead)

### Takeaway
Incumbent AI receptionists sell **directly to SMBs for about $29–$349/month**, and Smith.ai's AI tier costs about $1.60–$2.00 per call. **Wholesale voice infrastructure costs roughly $0.07–$0.20/min all-in** on Vapi and Retell. No churn rates are public. Saturation signs: YC-funded vertical players, many white-label "AI receptionist agency" kits, and self-serve price floors as low as $29–$59.

#### Legal: TCPA and AI voice
- [INDEP, law firms] **FCC Declaratory Ruling of 2024-02-08** (unanimous):
  - AI-generated and cloned voices are **"artificial or prerecorded voice"** under the TCPA, so calls made with them need the called party's **prior express consent** unless an emergency or exemption applies.
  - The caller must identify itself, and marketing calls must offer an opt-out.
  - The ruling took effect immediately.
  - Enforcement: FCC fines, possible blocking of carriers, private lawsuits, and suits by state attorneys general.
  - Sources: [Hunton](https://huntonak.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/fcc-issues-declaratory-ruling-that-tcpa-applies-to-ai-generated-voice-calls); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-02-15-fcc-ai-generated-robocalls-illegal-under-the-tcpa); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/fcc-requires-consent-for-ai-generated-cloned-voice-calls/); [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/amp/articles/2056830)
- [INDEP] An **August 2024 FCC proposal** would require AI callers to disclose AI use at the start of each call, and would let existing prerecorded-call consent cover AI calls only with clear disclosure. Whether it was finalized is not confirmed. — [Wiley](https://wiley.law/alert-FCC-Proposes-New-Rules-for-AI-Generated-Calls-and-Texts); [TCPAWorld](https://tcpaworld.com/2024/08/12/new-fcc-ai-regulations-the-fcc-adopts-mandatory-disclosure-requirements-for-ai-generated-calls-and-texts/)
- [INDEP] The FCC's **one-to-one consent rule was vacated** by the Eleventh Circuit on **2025-01-24** (Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC), one business day before it was due to take effect. Existing TCPA consent duties remain. — [Morrison Foerster](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule); [Kaufman Dolowich](https://www.kaufmandolowich.com/news-resources/eleventh-circuit-vacates-the-federal-communications-commissions-one-to-one-consent-rule-by-richard-perr-monica-littman-dominic-borelli-tabitha-mangano-and-kristen-ruotolo-3-5-2025/)

- [INDEP] Consent-revocation rules:
  - Most took effect **2025-04-11**: consumers may revoke by any reasonable means, and opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days.
  - The "revoke-all" provision is delayed to **2027-01-31**. — [Orrick (Jan 2026)](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-01-16/fcc-extends-robocall-consent-revocation-rule-waiver-to-january-2027/); [Burr & Forman](https://www.burr.com/telephone-consumer-protection-act/the-fcc-delays-effective-date-of-tcpa-revoke-all-rule-until-january-31-2027)

    - Senders may designate one exclusive opt-out channel.
    - An opt-out from an informational message may cover only that category.

    - Source: [Day Pitney](https://daypitney.com/the-fcc-changes-certain-consent-revocation-rules)
- [INDEP] In **McLaughlin Chiropractic v. McKesson (June 2025)**, the Supreme Court held that district courts are not bound by FCC interpretations of the TCPA. That makes litigation outcomes less predictable. — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-07-01-mclaughlin-chiropractic-us-supreme-court-invites-new-era-of-tcpa-jurisprudence)

### Inferences
- **Unit economics** (my arithmetic; assumes 200 calls × 3 min = 600 min/month for one client):
  - Usage-based cost at 8–15¢/min plus a ~$2 number is about **$50–$92/month**. At **$297** that leaves a gross margin of **~69–83%**; at **$497**, **~81–90%**. Support and setup time are not included.
  - On Trillet's flat **$299 + $0.12/min**, one client costs ~$371, more than a $297 price. Three clients give ~42% margin and five give ~56%.
  - **Synthflow's $2,000/month white-label tier** needs about 7–10 clients at $297 just to cover the fee, which is impossible within a ~$1,100 budget.
- **The price premium has to be earned.** An SMB using 600 minutes can self-serve on Rosie's $149 plan (1,000 minutes) or Dialzara's $199 plan (500 minutes plus overage). A reseller at $297–$497 must therefore justify a 2–3x premium through custom integration: CRM and calendar, lead routing, follow-up SMS, and monthly reporting tied to booked jobs. This fits a full-stack/n8n developer better than generic resale.
- **Legal posture.** This is not legal advice; verify with counsel.
  - Inbound answering is the lower-risk use case. The TCPA consent rules target calls made with an artificial voice.
  - Outbound AI speed-to-lead calls need documented prior express consent captured on the lead form, and written consent for marketing.
  - Every call should open with an AI disclosure and a recording/transcription disclosure, because callers may be in any of the 11–13 all-party states.
- **Avoid clinics at first.** HIPAA add-ons ($1–2k/month on some platforms), a full BAA chain, and his own business-associate liability make clinics a poor fit for under $1,100 of capital and 10–15 h/week.
- **Delivering from Romania.** Remote delivery is feasible, since all the tooling is cloud-based. Support has to cover US business hours, which fall in the Romanian afternoon and evening; this is a fit consideration, not sourced.

## 5. Micro-SaaS reality: time to first revenue, MRR distribution, share reaching $1k MRR

### Takeaway
Among indie SaaS products with **any** revenue in public TrustMRR-based datasets (2026), **median MRR is about $136–$145**, and only about a quarter to a third reach $1k MRR. MicroConf's 2024 survey covers only founders already earning revenue (a survivor sample). In it, **17.7% are under $1k MRR**, the single largest bracket, and **full-time founders grow 2.2x faster than part-timers**. I found **no credible median "time to first revenue"** for solo founders. Stripe Atlas reports that **about 1 in 5 incorporated startups gets a first paying customer within 30 days**, a selected population.

### Cited Findings
- [INDEP survey, 2024 edition; fieldwork late 2023] MicroConf **State of Independent SaaS 2024**:
  - Just under 700 usable responses; **pre-revenue builders excluded**.
  - The largest MRR bracket is **under $1,000, at 17.7%** of respondents (p. 34).
  - **50.7% are solo founders.**
  - **Full-time founders grow 2.2x faster** than part-time founders.
  - Companies whose entry plan costs $1,000+ add about **$1,073 of new MRR a month**, versus about **$316** for $1–9 entry plans.
  - Sources: [MicroConf State of Independent SaaS](https://microconf.com/saas-this-year); [MicroConf $0–10k ARR page](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr); [Startups For the Rest of Us, ep. 721](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-721-7-key-takeaways-from-the-2024-state-of-independent-saas-report)
  - No 2025 edition was found; MicroConf's pages still cite the 2024 data.
- [INDEP dataset, 2026] BigIdeasDB:
  - Across 3,787 indie SaaS businesses with real, non-zero revenue (TrustMRR-based), **median MRR is $145** and the mean is $4,298, pulled up by a few outliers. — [BigIdeasDB State of Indie SaaS Revenue 2026](https://bigideasdb.com/state-of-indie-saas-revenue-2026)
  - Across 5,000+ TrustMRR startups, a founder above $1,000 MRR outranks about two-thirds of revenue-earning startups. But its own bands put the top-quartile line at $880, which implies at least three-quarters are below $1k. Median about $136. — [BigIdeasDB SaaS Revenue Benchmarks 2026](https://bigideasdb.com/trustmrr-saas-revenue-benchmarks-2026)
- [ANECDOTE / unnamed analysis] An Indie Hackers thread cites about 5% of products above roughly $8,333/month, and notes that larger earners tend to stop posting revenue publicly. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-much-money-do-indie-hackers-products-make-74db9631f0)
- [OFFICIAL, company-reported] Stripe Atlas 2025 review (about 23,000 companies formed in 2025):
  - **About 1 in 5 Atlas companies wins its first paying customer within 30 days of formation**, more than double the 2020 share.
  - The time from formation to first revenue is shrinking.
  - Median revenue in the first six months rose 39% for the 2025 cohort vs 2024.
  - Sources: [Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/stripe-atlas-startups-in-2025-year-in-review); [Patrick Collison dispatch](https://patrickcollison.com/dispatches/atlas-year)
- [OFFICIAL] Stripe notes that founders **without a US Social Security number** can wait weeks or months for an EIN. Atlas now lets companies accept payments before the EIN arrives. — [Stripe: payments before EIN](https://stripe.com/blog/atlas-startups-can-now-accept-payments-prior-to-receiving-their-ein); [Stripe 2025 review](https://stripe.com/blog/stripe-atlas-startups-in-2025-year-in-review)
- [VENDOR] Freemius's "State of Micro-SaaS 2025" covers pricing-model revenue uplift, not MRR distribution. — [Freemius](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/)

### Inferences
- For a part-timer, the TrustMRR medians (about $140 MRR among products with any revenue) are a better starting assumption than MicroConf's survivors. The share of *all* launched side-project SaaS that ever reach $1k MRR is lower still, but I found no measurement of it.
- MicroConf's pricing data suggests higher entry prices add MRR much faster. Reaching $1k MRR within a year is far more plausible with 10–20 B2B customers at $50–$100/month than with hundreds of $5–$9 users. This is inferred.
- MicroConf's 2.2x full-time advantage implies a meaningful penalty at 10–15 h/week.
- The Atlas 30-day statistic describes incorporated startups that chose Atlas, not side projects, so it is not a benchmark for him. His **SSN** does remove the EIN delay Stripe describes for founders without one, which lowers the friction of setting up a US entity and payments (fit point).

### Gaps
- No median time to first revenue or to $1k MRR for solo or part-time founders.
- No SMB SaaS churn benchmarks; not searched because of the budget.
- MicroConf's MRR breakdown for solo founders, churn and time-to-profitability were not retrieved; the search was blocked.

## 6. Realistic income ranges at months 3, 6 and 12 (10–15 h/week), by model

### Takeaway
No study measures outcomes for **part-time (10–15 h/week) solo** operators in any of these models, so every range below is **my inference** from the cited anchors. These ranges are not findings. The most likely near-term cash is **freelancing**, on platforms or direct, built on AI-integration and full-stack skills: plausibly **~$1,000–$2,900/month by month 12** if it works. The two recurring models (retainers, receptionist resale) plausibly reach **low four figures of MRR by month 12 at best**. **Templates and micro-SaaS most likely earn under $100–$300/month in year one.** **$0 remains a realistic outcome for every model.**
