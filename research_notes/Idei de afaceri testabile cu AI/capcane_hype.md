## Q1. Per model: hard evidence on typical outcomes, platform/legal rule changes and failure rates

### Takeaway
From 2023 to 2026 every platform and regulator involved moved the same way. Monetisation thresholds went up, AI disclosure became mandatory, mass-produced and reused content lost monetisation, cheap non-EU imports picked up new fixed per-parcel costs, and "finfluencers" came under licensing pressure. The low-effort AI versions of these models are being closed off by the rules themselves, not only by competition. Hard failure-rate data exist mainly for trading (74–89% of retail CFD accounts lose money; 97% of persistent day traders lose) and in FTC case files on "AI-powered" e-commerce and agency schemes, where most buyers lost money. The popular "90% of dropshippers fail" statistics are unsourced vendor marketing.

#### 1.1 Faceless AI YouTube/TikTok channels, including the friend's idea (AI-edited re-uploads of viral clips as Shorts)

**YouTube monetisation policy**
- [PRESS] YouTube's YPP policy formerly called "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content", effective 15 July 2025. It targets mass-produced, template-like or repetitive uploads; examples given are story channels that differ only slightly and slideshow channels that share one narration. YouTube called it a minor update and said AI-made uploads remain monetisable when they add something original. The separate **reused-content** policy (compilations, commentary on others' material) was left unchanged. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [Plagiarism Today, 8 Jul 2025](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/)
- [PRESS/blog; not checked against YouTube's own page] A clarification dated 16 July 2026 reportedly sorts non-monetisable material into three buckets:
  - generic or repetitive videos;
  - off-putting or manipulative videos (e.g. dramatic animal-rescue clips);
  - AI personas discussing sensitive topics such as health or finance.

  Creators who keep uploading this material risk losing monetisation entirely. Coverage is split on whether this is a tidy-up or a new crackdown. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-updates-july-2026); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/youtube-bars-monetization-of-repetitive-manipulative-ai-videos-july-2026/tldr); [AIR Media-Tech timeline](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline)

#### 1.4 Dropshipping (EU/Romania, 2026)

**EU customs reform**
- [OFFICIAL] From **1 July 2026** the €150 customs-duty exemption ended. Business-to-consumer consignments worth €150 or less from outside the EU now pay a **flat €3 customs duty per tariff-classification item**: 5 identical T-shirts cost €3, while 3 T-shirts plus a watch cost €6. — [European Commission, 29 Jun 2026](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/ensuring-fairness-and-safety-eur3-customs-duty-low-value-parcels-2026-06-29_en); [Gerlach Customs](https://gerlach-customs.com/knowledge-base/eu-parcel-duty-150-exemption/)
- [VENDOR guides] Further details from commercial guides:
  - The legal basis is Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382, finally approved on 11 Feb 2026.
  - Liability sits with the seller, importer or their representative, not the buyer.
  - VAT is unchanged: the €150 line only ever covered customs duty, and the €3 is added on top of import VAT.
  - The measure is temporary until about July 2028 or the launch of the Customs Data Hub; end dates conflict between sources.

  — [Eurofiscalis](https://www.eurofiscalis.com/en/eu-3-euro-customs-duty-small-parcels/); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/europe/2025/11/eu-end-150-customs-duty-exemption-2026.html); [Flexfrontier](https://www.flexfrontier.com/news/eu-eu3-duty-on-low-value-parcels-now-live----duty-free-e-commerce-imports-are-over)
- [VENDOR; conflicting] Product-identifier data becomes mandatory from 1 Nov 2026. A €2 per-consignment handling fee from November 2026 is reported by one source and described as only "proposed" by another. IOSS handling after the reform is also contested between guides. — [Carra Globe](https://carraglobe.com/eu-de-minimis-changes-2026/); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/europe/2025/11/eu-end-150-customs-duty-exemption-2026.html)

**Romania's own parcel fee**
- [PRESS] From **1 January 2026**, Romania charges a **25-lei "taxă logistică" per non-EU parcel worth under €150** containing commercial goods sent to individuals.
  - The Finance Ministry calls it an "internal fiscal obligation", not a customs duty.
  - The legal payer is the non-EU seller or platform.
  - Poșta Română and couriers collect it and report monthly to ANAF.
  - It is not refunded on returns; undelivered parcels are excluded.

### Inferences
- **The friend's idea is blocked twice over** (inference from 1.1).
  - Re-uploading other creators' viral clips with AI edits is what the unchanged *reused-content* policy and the 2025–2026 *inauthentic-content* enforcement target.
  - Even if monetised, the Feb 2027 rules require a *new* channel to reach 20M Shorts views in 90 days to join YPP via Shorts, and 10M per 90 days just to share in the Shorts pool.
  - At the vendor-reported RPMs of $0.01–$0.06 per 1,000 Shorts views (Klap), 10M views per quarter pays about **$100–$600 per quarter**; even at AIR's US average of $0.328 it is about **$3,280 per quarter** (arithmetic).
  - Romanian-registered TikTok accounts appear unable to use Creator Rewards at all.
  - The copyright side (Content ID, strikes) was not researched here.
- **The AI-slop "success" figures are survivorship data.** Kapwing sampled only the ~15,000 top-trending channels, so its 278 slop channels and $117M estimate describe winners, not the typical faceless channel. Kapwing also sells the tools.
- **Low-ticket China-to-Romania dropshipping has lost its cost advantage.**
  - Since Jan/Jul 2026 a single-item parcel carries 25 lei (≈ €5, assuming about 5 lei/€; exchange rate not sourced) plus €3 duty, and possibly €2 more from November, before 21% VAT.
  - That is roughly €8–€10 of fixed fees on, say, a €15 item, about 53–67% of its value (arithmetic).
  - A Romania-established seller bringing goods into the EU is also likely the "importer" for GPSR purposes and carries RP-type duties (inference from 1.4; confirm with a customs/GPSR adviser).
- **The KDP cap is not the binding constraint.** Three titles a day still allows about 1,095 titles a year per account. The binding constraints are the disclosure requirement and the complete absence (in this pass) of evidence that AI-generated books sell.
- **SEO/affiliate content sites face two headwinds at once.** One is algorithmic: scaled-content-abuse policies apply whether or not AI is used. The other is structural: AI Overviews cut CTR by about 47–65% depending on study, and small publishers lost about 60% of search referrals over two years. Even the best-documented recovery (HouseFresh) took about 2 years with an expert team.
- **The trading base rates leave no evidence-backed case for paid signals or bots.** 73–89% of retail CFD accounts lose; 97% of persistent day traders lose; under 1% are reliably profitable. Selling signals also creates regulatory exposure (1.7).
- **The FTC record shows one consistent pattern.** Across Automators, Ascend, Ecommerce Empire Builders, FBA Machine, Click Profit, Growth Cave and Air AI, sellers of "AI-powered" income opportunities collected $15M–$25M per scheme while most buyers lost money.

## Q2. Who actually makes money, what happens to the median participant, and Romania/EU (plus US-citizen) legal and tax risks

### Takeaway
The money reliably goes to four groups: platforms (cuts, retained ad revenue, users kept on the search page), tool vendors, "done-for-you" and course sellers, and a handful of outliers. Documented outcomes for participants are poor. FTC records show most buyers losing money (zero-sale stores, suspended storefronts, losses up to $250k), and academic trading data show 97% of persistent day traders losing. Headline earnings are self-reported or estimated. For a Romania-resident developer, the concrete legal exposures are:
- unlicensed investment advice (ESMA/ASF);
- product-safety, consumer and new parcel-fee obligations for physical goods (GPSR, ANPC, 25 lei + €3);
- AI labelling (AI Act Art. 50, live since 2 Aug 2026; fines up to €15M/3%);
- tax self-declaration (16% on crypto gains from 2026; platforms reporting to ANAF).

US-citizen-specific obligations could not be verified in this pass.

## Q3. Which models have a legitimate, evidence-backed variant, and what separates it from the trap version

### Takeaway
A legitimate variant exists only where the participant **owns or licenses the underlying asset** (footage, design, product, expertise, client relationship) and is **paid by someone with a real business reason**. That means a rights holder paying for clips, a buyer paying for an original design or product, or a business paying for working automation, as opposed to platform arbitrage of other people's content or "selling the dream" to newcomers. The evidence supports:
- licensed clipping campaigns (real but low pay per view);
- original, AI-assisted content (allowed, but slow and threshold-gated);
- original-design POD with disclosures;
- first-hand, original-research content (slow; recovery rare).

Physical-goods e-commerce is legitimate only with EU-compliant supply chains, which strains a budget under 5,000 lei. Paid trading signals and "AI income" courses or agency licences have no legitimate low-capital version without authorisation or substantiated claims. Wrapper apps and AI-agency demand could not be evidenced in this pass.
